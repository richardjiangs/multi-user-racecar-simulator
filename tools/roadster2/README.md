# Roadster 2

Added October 2026. Only the new Roadster simulator uses these modules. The original 64 simulator sources and their performance/audio are preserved.

## References and scope

- [Tesla Roadster](https://www.tesla.com/roadster?redirect=no): current 2026 exterior, gold glazing, white fixed seats, yoke, two forward displays and center touchscreen; Performance, Thrust and Rocket variants. The cockpit was checked against Tesla's official carousel interior image. Vector artwork is authored here, not a recolored Plaid drawing. The driving view uses the low, full-width Type A composition and original footer controls; the yoke, fascia and screen details are Roadster-specific.
- [Golden Gate Bridge District dimensions](https://www.goldengate.org/bridge/history-research/statistics-data/design-construction-stats/): 2,737 m overall bridge, 1,280 m main span, 227 m towers, approximately 67 m deck clearance. Roadway and bridge geometry use those principal dimensions; terrain, skyline and surroundings are artistic approximations. The marked Golden Gate ramp is fictional simulator scenery, not a claimed Tesla test location.
- [Port Authority JFK runway diagram](https://www.panynj.gov/content/dam/port-authority/aircraft-noise/jfkac-presentation-sep-2022.pdf): runway 13R/31L nominal 14,511 × 200 ft (4,423 × 60.96 m). Terminal/apron scenery is an authored airport approximation, not a surveyed operational airport.
- Tesla Development Grounds is a fictional electric-car test oval. McGregor is a demo-inspired arena, not a survey of the SpaceX site. Its authored open C-profile has a 160 m uphill approach easing to 15°, a 22 m-radius U-bend, and the user-requested 2,000 m inverted straight ending in a free edge, with no return loop. The 2 km length and open end follow the user’s layout; entry/radius/height remain authored geometry, not a surveyed replica. The full mesh is built before driving; the identical arc-length geometry controls tyre contact and collisions. Tesla’s official Roadster page describes powered fan/seal suction separately from its cold-gas flight thrusters. Ramp suction targets a 1 g tyre preload with an estimated 2 g capacity, using normal acceleration and gravity in the road frame. Wheel drive/braking/steering respect the resulting tyre load. Z enables surface downforce and the electric seal. Cold-gas depletion does not switch off the electric fan; power loss can release an inverted car. At the open edge, the car retains its velocity and orientation and enters free flight. W/S cannot pitch tyres through a sealed surface; release Z before lifting off with P/L. Rocket can be switched off on the ramp without disabling Z suction. A correctly oriented airborne car can attach to the driving face from either direction; within 8 m, Z brakes the normal approach before tyre capture.

The side elevation uses revised wheelbase/overhang proportions, separate titanium thin-spoke wheels with brake hardware, gold canopy reflections, white panel facets, lamp optics, body seams and animated perimeter thrusters.

Detailed A350-family aircraft use [Airbus published dimensions](https://www.airbus.com/en/products-services/commercial-aircraft/passenger-aircraft/a350-family): 73.78 m A350-1000 length, 64.75 m span, and 66.8 m A350-900 length. Fuselage cross-sections, swept cambered wings, nacelles with visible fan blades, cockpit/window/door details, lights and retractable landing-gear meshes are authored locally. Static meshes are uploaded once and rigidly transformed along continuous scenery flight paths; these are not real air-traffic data.

Steering uses estimated 2.75 m wheelbase and 35° maximum road-wheel lock, with speed-sensitive ground grip. Moving flight turns use a matching low-speed radius with acceleration limits; I/O rotation stays independent. These values are not published production steering specifications.

Precise production mass, drivetrain output and gas endurance are not fully published. The game explicitly labels its 1,800 kg, 900 kW, 400 km/h ceiling and 300,000 N·s stored gas impulse as **simulation estimates**, not factory specifications. Flight uses SI gravity, force/mass acceleration, drag, gas impulse consumption, attitude stabilization and landing velocity. It is a game flight model, not flight-test validation.

## Controls

- Z: toggle downforce and surface grip. Z blocks flight lift; nearby tyre-side inverted surfaces attract and capture the car. V independently toggles the rear jet on/off; held-key repeats do not toggle it again. Brakes override the jet while applied.
- Y: toggle Rocket / SpaceX without position hold, auto-landing or a change in momentum. Arming releases Z downforce. F and the Land button are explicit separate actions.
- Rocket mode only: Tab increases the throttle lever by 5 percentage points per press; Shift decreases it. Holding a key does not repeat the adjustment. Release keeps the selected throttle. Up/down arrows are temporary accelerator/brake pedals; braking overrides but does not reset the lever.
- A/D set the roll yoke in degrees, one degree of yoke per degree of roll; release holds that angle. It accepts angles beyond 360°. W/S pitch about the body-right axis, so inverted W points toward the ground and a 90° bank makes pitch turn sideways. Thrust, camera and rear view follow the same orthonormal body frame. A balanced thruster couple rotates the body without a sideways force or a heading change. Left/right arrows, horizontal touchpad swipes (including fullscreen capture) and the touch wheel turn the nose and trajectory about the car’s current body-up axis, including inverted and vertical flight.
- P/L command vertical hover up/down. W/S pitch the nose up/down without an angular stop: forward thrust follows the nose, allowing climbs, dives and full loops. W can lift a moving car; it cannot act as the ground accelerator in Rocket mode.
- I/O turn the nose faster than ordinary steering, without braking or resetting throttle. At rest they rotate on the spot; when moving they rotate three-dimensional velocity with the nose, preserving speed. Their estimated path-acceleration ceiling is 72 m/s², twice ordinary flight steering, with a 3 rad/s low-speed cap. With F hold explicitly engaged, I/O rotates while that hold remains active.
- F restores the saved neutral up direction, centers the bank yoke, zeros forward throttle and holds a fixed horizontal position and altitude. Increasing throttle or applying normal steering or pitch releases position hold. A/D roll and I/O turn-in-place can be used while holding position. P/L can adjust its height. G rebases the yoke to zero without rotating or translating the car; the next A/D input starts from this new neutral, which F also respects. The reset cancels any previous attitude correction. F aligns body-up using the shortest rotation rather than unwinding Euler angles; a half backflip followed by a 180° roll does not make F rotate an already upright car. Landing explicitly levels to world-up, and touchdown resets the hover neutral. The Land button brakes to a fixed position and descends. Disarming restores normal W/S and A/D road controls. The shared-track input boundary and capturing key listeners both enforce this separation.
- G+2 (either key order, including keypad 2): centers the yoke and aligns only roll to the current track/surface normal. The nose vector, pitch and momentum stay unchanged; a backwards-facing car remains backwards-facing, including on later touchdown. The resulting attitude becomes the G/F neutral. Shared-road banking/grade and contacted inverted ramp normals are used. At a perfectly vertical nose, track-relative roll is undefined, so it keeps the bank and centers only. A matching touch button is in the controls drawer.
- Cockpit D READY: shut doors, buckle, power on, select drive. Individual belt, power, neutral, reverse, lights and door controls also work.
- REFILL services a stopped car on the ground. Real Mode consumes gas and preserves damage; accessible mode replenishes gas and has no persistent impact damage.
- Touch controls are in the Roadster controls drawer. The existing steering wheel, D-pad and immediate T+Space trackpad controls remain available.

Flight works in the four complete Roadster worlds in both editions and over shared Track 2.0 worlds. Legacy pseudo-3D Track 1.0 circuits keep ground driving; their launch prompt directs the driver to a flight arena or Track 2.0.

Only Rocket enables flight key bindings or the degree-yoke display, including after a jump or landing. Ordinary airborne jumps do not arm those controls. The flight speedometer measures the full three-dimensional velocity, including climbing speed; braking opposes all three components. Full-throttle flight uses the same drag law in every direction. W/S bends both the nose and the flight path using vectored normal thrust; the rotation preserves speed, and loops grow in radius as speed rises to respect the estimated 36 m/s² path-acceleration limit. Normal-thrust impulse is included in Real Mode gas consumption. A/D roll does not turn velocity. F alone brakes to a fixed point; I/O preserves the selected throttle. Braking, loss of thrust or a collision can reduce speed; pitching through a loop does not artificially turn forward thrust into repeated braking.

The special-world road touchpad springs back after a swipe, matching shared tracks; only Rocket A/D is a persistent degree yoke. Golden Gate terrain collision uses the exact rendered island/headland triangles. Water slows sinking to at most 0.5 m/s, allows powered escape, and recovers the car only when its entire oriented body is submerged, in both Real Mode settings. Flying beneath the bridge does not snap the car onto its deck.

Rocket-off arrow steering has priority over a quiet fullscreen trackpad, assisted line or test driver in the Roadster arenas, matching the shared-circuit input priority.

## Build and validation

1. `node tools/roadster2/build.mjs`
2. `python3 tools/build-track2.py`
3. Serve repo on localhost:8000; `node tools/roadster2/snapshot.mjs`
4. `node --max-old-space-size=8192 tools/embed-sims.mjs`
5. `node tests/roadster2/controls.mjs` and `node tests/roadster2/controls.mjs --garage` (real keyboard, slider, touch wheel and fullscreen steering, both physics paths)
6. `node tests/roadster2/ground-arrows.mjs` and `node tests/roadster2/roll.mjs` (keyboard priority and continuous independent roll)
7. `node tests/roadster2/hover-yaw.mjs` (fixed hover, unlimited yaw, moving/ground U-turns and aircraft motion)
8. `node tests/roadster2/flight.mjs` and `node tests/roadster2/arenas.mjs` (worlds, gas, landings, ramps and touch controls)

The builder clones the existing EV template for compatibility, then installs unique art, controls, audio and worlds. Unused donor dashboard and body drawings are removed during generation. Each world mesh is constructed completely before driving and uploaded once per selection; the render loop does not generate chunks in front of the car. All new assets are procedural or vector source, with no remote runtime dependency. The LITE edition retains its specified ten cars.

9. `node tests/roadster2/connected-flight.mjs` verifies G, steady climbs, unarmed jumps, inverted grip, independent gas/suction power and the open ramp exit. `node tests/roadster2/live-stability.mjs` exercises live animation, every route, all map modes, views and mixed flight inputs.

Individual offline files delegate fullscreen explicitly to their `srcdoc` simulator, including opaque `file://` origins. Run `node tests/refinements/individual-fullscreen.mjs` to verify actual fullscreen entry and Escape (both chord orders) in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit; checking only `trackpadSteering` does not verify fullscreen.

The special-world canvas skips rendering while its viewport has zero width or height, including rear-camera and sky passes. A zero-sized WebGL canvas cannot be copied with `drawImage`; an uncaught error there used to terminate the animation loop. `tests/roadster2/viewport-recovery.mjs` collapses and restores each dimension across all four arenas and all GPS modes, and requires live animation to resume. `offline-special-stability.mjs` exercises the original and compressed Downloads versions with Real Mode off through extended control/landing/map transitions.

`node tests/roadster2/hints-ramp.mjs` checks toast expiry across both physics paths, each arena and flight, plus inverted stopping/reversing, contact-force balance, empty-gas grip, power-off release and tangent flight at the open edge. Set `ROADSTER_TEST_FILE` to test the actual compressed Downloads file. Roadster custom physics advances the original toast timer exactly once; the donor physics handles its own timer.

`node tests/roadster2/flight-momentum.mjs` compares 45 seconds of full-throttle straight flight with repeated upright, inverted and sideways loops, roll, mixed roll/pitch and moving turns. It checks actual three-dimensional speed, nose-relative velocity, bounded path acceleration, brakes and gas exhaustion in every Roadster arena and shared Track 2.0. `ROADSTER_TEST_FILE` exercises the compressed Downloads file.

`node tests/roadster2/contact-controls.mjs` checks real swipe/key events, ground wheel return, V toggling, Y isolation, faster I/O with full throttle, Z takeoff inhibition, direct inverted landing in both headings/modes, water escape and full-submersion recovery, and terrain landing/driving. Set `ROADSTER_TEST_FILE` for the final compressed Downloads preview.

`node tests/roadster2/neutral-frame.mjs` checks the reported backflip → roll upright → G → F sequence, tilted reference resets, G during hover correction, body-relative pitch, and all steering sources at 0/90/180/270/720° roll and vertical flight. Both editions and Real Mode settings are covered; `ROADSTER_TEST_FILE` tests the compressed standalone file.
